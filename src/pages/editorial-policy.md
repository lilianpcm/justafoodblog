Every food list on Just A Food Blog is built from published dietary guidance and checked before it goes live. This page explains how.

## Where the information comes from

We rely on government health institutes, national health services, academic medical centers and medical societies, such as the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK), the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the NHS, the Mayo Clinic and the professional society for each condition. Every page lists the sources it used, so you can read them yourself.

## How a food list is made

1. We collect the dietary guidance for the condition from the sources above.
2. Each food is placed in one of three groups: **eat**, **limit** (with a portion) or **avoid**.
3. When sources disagree or the evidence is weak, we choose the more cautious option.
4. We use AI tools to help research and draft. Every list and every page is then cross-checked against the listed sources in a separate, independent review before it is published.

## What we don't do

- We never claim that a food or a diet cures, treats or prevents a condition.
- We don't name brands or sell supplements.
- We don't give personal medical advice. Diets vary from person to person, and some conditions need a plan made with your doctor or a registered dietitian.

## Updates and corrections

Each page shows the date it was last updated. We review pages when the guidance changes or when readers point out something unclear. Found a mistake? Write to {{ site.contact_email }} and we will check it.
